TAIL_180_BEFORE_MARKER: "**Why the project is worthwhile and achievable.** It is worthwhile because it is the shape of real work: an IT team of five people supporting 160 staff across four locations, under a contractual obligation to prove least privilege, auditability and failover, with no budget for new hardware — which is exactly the situation in which a well-designed virtualised estate, reviewed configuration-as-code and honest documentation are the difference between a system that can be defended in an audit and one that cannot. It is achievable within twelve weeks by four students with no physical hardware for four reasons, and each was designed in rather than assumed. **First, the scope was prioritised before the work began**, in a fixed four-band order with a pre-agreed cut sequence, so that if the schedule compresses it compresses predictably and the must band survives intact. **Second, the phases follow the service dependency chain**, so at most one phase is ever blocked and four people can genuinely work in parallel instead of queueing. **Third, the highest-uncertainty item is isolated and timeboxed**: the innovation line's runtime is exposed to a three-day feasibility spike whose output is a decision, and it is protected by designed fallbacks, so the least certain part of the project cannot take the rest of it down. **Fourth, and most importantly, the verification and documentation discipline is built into every task rather than added at the end** — evidence is part of the definition of done, the pairing arrangement means no member is a single point of failure, and the traceability matrix is maintained continuously, so that week 12 assembles a record that already exists rather than reconstructing one from memory. The project's honesty rules are part of the same design: version deviations are published, declared exclusions are listed and approved, the simulator's limits are labelled wherever a result touches them, and where the innovation line disappoints, the disappointment is documented as a finding. Delivering the must band with complete evidence, and stating plainly and precisely what was not delivered and why, is a complete and defensible outcome for a twelve-week final-year project — and it is the outcome this plan is built to produce"
